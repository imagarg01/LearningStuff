## 2. The 5 Specific Habits You Must Build to Fill Your Gaps

### Gap-Filler #1: The "Rule of Page 1 Valuation" (Fixing Your Module 5 Blind Spot)
**New Personal Rule:** You are never allowed to write more than 1 paragraph about a company's business model until you write down this exact **5-Line Valuation Box** at the top of your page:
1. **Current Stock Price & Market Cap:** (e.g., ₹320 / ₹13,800 Cr)
2. **Trailing 12-Month (TTM) Revenue, EBITDA & PAT:** (e.g., ₹1,175 Cr Rev / ₹680 Cr EBITDA / ₹600 Cr PAT)
3. **Current P/E, EV/EBITDA & FCF Yield vs. 5-Year Historical Median:** (e.g., 23x P/E today vs. 50x at IPO)
4. **Reverse Expectations Math:** *"If exit P/E in Year 3 is $X$ (be conservative: max 25x–30x), how much must PAT grow every year for me to earn 20% CAGR on my stock?"*
5. **Verdict Before Deep Research:** Is the required PAT growth (say 35% CAGR at 50x P/E) realistic for this industry? If not, **stop right there** and put the stock on a watchlist to wait for a 30–40% price/time correction!

### Gap-Filler #2: The "Cross-Module Contradiction Hunt" (Fixing Your Module 2 & 4 Blind Spot)
Whenever you write a bullish claim in **Module 1 (Business Model)**, immediately try to **disprove your own claim** using **Module 2 (Balance Sheet/Cash Flow)**:

* **Example A (From IGIL):**
  * *Module 1 Claim:* "Customers are hostage payers; IGIL holds the diamond as collateral until paid."
  * *Module 2 Check:* "Then why are Receivable/Working Capital days at 113 days?"
  * *Discovery:* Because in the In-Factory B2B model, they give credit to keep volume flowing during a customer liquidity crisis!
* **Example B (Pricing Power vs. Gross Margin / ARP):**
  * *Module 1 Claim:* "Company has a monopoly moat and inelastic pricing power."
  * *Module 2/4 Check:* "What happened to Average Realized Price (ARP) or Gross Margin over the last 8 quarters?"
  * *Discovery (From IGIL):* ARP actually fell 12% in early 2024 (from ₹1,081 to ₹950) and another 2.6% in CY25 (to ₹927) to defend market share! That proves pricing power has a ceiling when the underlying commodity (LGD) crashes in value.

### Gap-Filler #3: The "Customer Health Ceiling" Test (Value Chain Reality Check)
In B2B businesses (like IGIL serving diamond polishers, or an auto-ancillary serving carmakers, or a chemical firm serving agrochemical giants):
* **Never project margin expansion in isolation.**
* Ask: **What is the EBITDA margin and ROCE of my company's customers?**
* If your company is making **60% EBITDA margin and 58% ROCE**, while its customers are making **3% EBITDA margin and 6% ROCE** and struggling to survive, **your company cannot raise prices or expand margins further without killing its own customers!** Any efficiency gains (like AI in IGIL) will have to be shared with the customer just to keep the ecosystem alive.

---

### Gap-Filler #4: Following the Cash in IPOs & M&A ("Who Actually Gets the Money?")
Whenever a company raises money (IPO/QIP) or announces an acquisition ("Reverse Inversion / Global Consolidation"):
* Draw a simple box showing **Who Sends Cash** and **Whose Bank Account Receives Cash**.
* In IGIL's IPO, retail investors saw "₹2,750 Cr OFS + ₹1,475 Cr Fresh Issue" and assumed ₹1,475 Cr was going into growing the business.
* Tracing the cash one step further revealed that **₹1,300 Cr of the ₹1,475 Cr Fresh Issue was wired to Blackstone's private holding company** to buy low-margin European subsidiaries. Always check:
  1. Is the seller in an acquisition a **Related Party / Promoter / PE Parent**?
  2. Does the acquired entity have **lower ROCE and lower margins** than the listed Indian entity? (If yes, consolidated ROCE and valuation multiples will de-rate post-merger!).

### Gap-Filler #5: Checking the "Shareholder Supply Calendar" Before Buying (Module 6)
Before buying any stock—especially one listed in the last 5 years or backed by PE/VC:
1. Open the **Shareholding Pattern**.
2. Add up the total % held by **Private Equity, Venture Capital, and Pre-IPO Financial Investors**.
3. If that number is **> 10%** (in IGIL's case, it is **76.6%!**), write in bold red letters on your note: **"HEAVY INSTITUTIONAL SUPPLY OVERHANG."**
4. Do **not** buy during Stage 3 (after a run-up) or Stage 4 (while the stock is sliding below its 200-DMA). Wait until either:
   * Valuation compresses so much (like IGIL dropping from 50x to 23x P/E) that earnings yield protects your downside, **AND**
   * Price-volume action enters **Stage 2** (breaking above the 200-DMA after absorbing block deals).
